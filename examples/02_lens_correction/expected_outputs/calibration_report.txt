=== Interpretation ===
Note: this section is an interpretation of the numbers above, written by
GeoCamPal from fixed rules. It is not a measurement. Check it against your
own use before acting on it.

Does the lens distort the image?
  - The largest movement (2.72 px) is smaller than 2 times its ± (3.75 px).
    These photos do not show a lens distortion.
  - They also cannot rule one out. A distortion of up to about 10 px at the
    top-left corner would not have been noticed (largest movement + 2 × its
    ±). For half of all pixels the ± is 0.35 px or less.

Where does the misfit come from?
  - The distortion model removes 0.04 % of the misfit. The remaining 1.75 px
    (0.057 % of the image width) comes from the board or the photos, not from
    lens distortion that this model can describe.
  - On the board, the remaining misfit is 0.54 mm on average. 0.39 mm of that
    is the same in every photo, which points to the pattern itself. 0.38 mm
    changes from photo to photo, which points to the board bending, to blur,
    or to how exactly the corners were found.

What it means for your images (3088 × 2064 px)
  - If this file is applied, pixels move by at most 2.72 px (0.09 % of the
    image width), and half of all pixels by no more than 0.04 px.
  - Applying the file and not applying it are both consistent with these
    photos.
  - Example: if one pixel covers 10 cm on the ground, 2.72 px is 27 cm and the
    10 px that cannot be ruled out is 100 cm, both at the top-left corner. For
    half of all pixels the ± is 3.5 cm or less.
  - The grade limits are in pixels. A smaller image size gives a smaller RMS
    in pixels for the same board, so the grade can improve without a better
    calibration. The share of the image width (0.057 %) does not change with
    the image size; use it to compare calibrations.

Limits of this interpretation
  - The ± comes from repeating the calibration 18 times, each time without one
    photo. An error that is the same in every photo, such as an inexact
    printed pattern, is not part of it. In tests with such an error, the true
    error was up to 4 times the ±.
  - More photos make the ± smaller, but not an error of the board.

Suggestions
  - To find out whether this lens distorts, use a pattern on a flat, stiff
    backing and check the printed cell size with a ruler.
  - Turn the board by 90° and 180° in some of the photos and place it in all
    parts of the frame. A board error then no longer looks like lens
    distortion.
  - Use "k1 only", as the model guidance suggests.
  - If you apply the file, check that straight lines near the image edges stay
    straight in the corrected images.
